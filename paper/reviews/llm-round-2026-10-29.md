## Points raised by more than one reviewer

- **§6.2 "time follows the size of the removed set, not the size of the store" is stronger than
  the evidence** (ChatGPT, Mistral, Claude): one run per size, about 1.6x run-to-run drift
  reported, two large data points.
- **§6.5 "the provenance cost is the same under both generators"**: the table's maxima are 26 vs
  36 (Mistral, Claude). §4.4 also cites observed maxima.
- **§6.3 query latency**: the reified join was faster (0.56 vs 0.66 ms median), and named graphs
  were counted but not queried, so this is a storage comparison (ChatGPT, Claude, Mistral).
- **Retraction semantics**: one justification per triple means over-deletion when duplicate
  writers merge citation sets, and there is no statement-level retraction (ChatGPT, Mistral,
  Claude W8). The paper already says this in §7.2.
- **No external baseline** for retraction or provenance (ChatGPT's novelty table, Mistral W1,
  Claude W2: compare with a SPARQL `inferredFrom+` property-path closure).
- **Evaluation breadth**: one laptop, single runs, no repeated trials (ChatGPT, Mistral W5, Claude W4).
